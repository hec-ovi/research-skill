## Summary

Salt substitutes significantly reduced systolic blood pressure by 4.8 mmHg across 23 randomized controlled trials (32,073 participants) published through March 2022. The largest trial (SSaSS, China, 20,995 participants, 5-year follow-up) demonstrated reductions in stroke (14%), major cardiovascular events (13%), and all-cause mortality (12%). Subgroup analyses showed no significant differences by age or sample size, but longer trials demonstrated greater benefits. Cardiovascular risk reduction estimated at approximately 2% per 1 mmHg systolic blood pressure reduction suggests the observed 4.8 mmHg reduction would prevent approximately 10% of major cardiovascular events.

**Overall Significance of Findings for Blood Pressure Control and Mortality**

The meta-analyses demonstrated consistent and significant blood pressure reductions with salt substitutes. Across 23 RCTs (32,073 participants), the mean systolic blood pressure reduction was 4.80 mmHg (95% CI −6.12 to −3.48), and diastolic blood pressure reduction was 1.48 mmHg. These reductions achieved statistical significance and were described as clinically meaningful.

Most critically, the SSaSS trial (Neal 2021) involving 20,995 participants in rural China with a mean follow-up of 4.74 years reported major clinical outcomes: stroke rate 29.14 per 1,000 person-years with salt substitute versus 33.65 with regular salt (14% risk reduction); major cardiovascular events 49.09 versus 56.29 per 1,000 person-years (13% reduction); and total mortality 39.27 versus 44.61 per 1,000 person-years (12% reduction). No increased risk of serious adverse events attributed to clinical hyperkalemia was observed in the salt substitute group.

A long-term follow-up study (Sun 2021) in rural North China tracked 428 participants over 10 years. Among all participants, stroke mortality was reduced by 74% (HR = 0.26), and among hypertensive patients specifically, cardiovascular disease mortality was reduced by 62% (HR = 0.38).

Meta-analytic evidence confirmed these findings: analyses of 21 trials with approximately 32,000 participants across 15 countries showed a risk ratio of 0.89 (95% CI 0.85–0.94) for all-cause mortality (approximately 11% reduction), a risk ratio of 0.87 (95% CI 0.81–0.94) for cardiovascular mortality (approximately 13% reduction), and a risk ratio of 0.89 (95% CI 0.85–0.94) for major cardiovascular events (approximately 11% reduction).

**Subgroup Analyses**

*Age (<60 years vs. ≥60 years):* Subgroup analyses by age did not show statistically significant differences in blood pressure reduction. However, elderly participants in nutrition education interventions (which overlap with salt reduction studies in some contexts) showed slightly numerically greater systolic reductions (approximately 5.94 mmHg in those ≥60 years). The primary salt substitute studies showed no major heterogeneity by age group for blood pressure outcomes.

*Sample Size (<300 vs. ≥300 participants):* Results did not suggest different effects of salt substitution on blood pressure when subgrouping was performed by sample size (< 300 vs. ≥300 participants). Both smaller and larger trials demonstrated consistent blood pressure reductions.

*Study Duration (<12 months vs. ≥12 months):* Trial duration was associated with outcome differences. Meta-regression analysis indicated that longer-duration trials demonstrated greater cardiovascular benefits. The SSaSS trial (4.75 years) and Sun 2021 follow-up (10 years) reported the most substantial reductions in mortality and cardiovascular events, suggesting that longer exposure to salt substitutes yields cumulative cardiovascular benefits.

---

**Cardiovascular Risk Reduction Based on Observed Systolic Blood Pressure Lowering**

The relationship between systolic blood pressure reduction and cardiovascular risk reduction is approximately linear, with epidemiologic evidence indicating roughly 2% reduction in cardiovascular risk per 1 mmHg systolic blood pressure reduction. This relationship derives from meta-analyses showing that each 5-mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure reduces cardiovascular event risk by approximately 10%, and each 10-mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure reduces stroke risk by approximately 27%.

Given the observed mean systolic blood pressure reduction of 4.8 mmHg from salt substitutes:
- Estimated cardiovascular event risk reduction: 4.8 mmHg × 2% per mmHg = approximately 9.6% relative risk reduction in major cardiovascular events
- This aligns closely with the observed meta-analytic risk ratio of 0.89 (11% reduction) for major cardiovascular events

For the largest trial (SSaSS): the baseline mean systolic blood pressure was 153.8 mmHg, and although exact BP reductions were not uniformly reported, the 14% stroke reduction observed translates to a blood pressure reduction of approximately 7 mmHg (using the 27% reduction per 10 mmHg stroke risk relationship). This suggests the salt substitute (75% NaCl, 25% KCl) achieved somewhat greater blood pressure reduction than the meta-analytic average, consistent with its larger effect size for cardiovascular events.

## Insights

- **Composition effects:** Trials using potassium chloride-containing substitutes (representing 23 of 26 trials in the Cochrane review) consistently showed blood pressure reductions. Each 10% reduction in sodium chloride content of the salt substitute was associated with 1.5 mmHg greater systolic blood pressure reduction, suggesting dose-response relationships.

- **Trial duration and cumulative effects:** Longer trials demonstrated greater absolute reductions in cardiovascular events and mortality, with the 10-year follow-up study reporting 74% stroke mortality reduction. This suggests salt substitutes may prevent cumulative vascular damage over time beyond the immediate blood pressure effect.

- **Consistency across populations:** Despite heterogeneity in baseline characteristics, age groups, and study settings (Asia, Europe, South America), the blood pressure-lowering effect remained remarkably consistent (mean 4.8 mmHg), suggesting the intervention is robust across diverse populations.

- **Synergy with medication:** Sub-analyses of the SSaSS trial showed cardiovascular benefits were significant only among participants taking antihypertensive medications, with 19% reduction in major adverse events and 17% reduction in all-cause mortality. This suggests salt substitutes may work synergistically with pharmacologic therapy.

- **Safety profile:** Multiple trials across different populations and follow-up durations reported no significant increase in serious adverse events from hyperkalemia, despite the potassium content of substitutes. This supports feasibility for public health implementation.

- **Geographic variation in results:** Chinese trials (which constituted the majority of study participants due to the large SSaSS and related studies) showed stronger cardiovascular outcome benefits than Western shorter-duration trials, possibly reflecting greater baseline salt intake in the Chinese population and longer exposure to the intervention.

## Strongest objection

One smaller trial (Bernabe-Ortiz, Peru) showed more modest blood pressure reductions (1.29 mmHg systolic, 0.76 mmHg diastolic) than the meta-analytic average, which could reflect lower baseline salt intake in rural Peruvian communities or implementation barriers in discretionary salt use settings.
